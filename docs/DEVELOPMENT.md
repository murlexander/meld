# Meld development

A small native Mac playground for abstract colour studies and painting inspiration.

## Try it

Open `Meld.app`. Each session starts with source selection; there is no sample artwork or manual workspace on launch.

1. Choose a folder of photographs or textures. The previous folder is offered without scanning at startup. Pick **Square**, **Portrait**, or **Landscape**.
2. **Generate 36 previews** (⌘R). The main window becomes a grid. Click to select and double-click (or Return) to refine. Arrow keys move the selection; **F** picks and **D** unpicks, matching Louppe's Yes/No keys. Stars also mark picks, and the **Picks** toggle filters them. ⌘+ and ⌘− change the thumbnail size (130–250 points; previews stay sharp on Retina up to 240).
   **Variations** (V, ⌥⌘R, or the context menu) adds a row of 12 relatives of the selected or open preview, including its adjustments. The row runs from close relatives, which nudge every setting including the stages, to bolder ones, which may swap in another session image, a pattern shape or a blend, or swap one stage for another of its family (a different colour treatment, mask, mirror axis or blur) while the rest stays. Variations reuse decoded images, so they never read the source folder.
3. Refinement opens a larger, zoomable image with four controls: **Distortion**, **Pattern amount**, **Colour**, and **Contrast**. Distortion, pattern amount and colour scale the generated recipe, leaving any value edited under More controls as set: distortion scales the warps, one-axis stretch and blur, and switches mirror symmetry off at 0; pattern amount scales the patterns (never above the 30% texture limit) and grain; colour scales saturation and the colour treatment's mix (0 is greyscale with no treatment). Contrast adjusts the output. Double-click a slider to reset it to its generated default.
   ⌘[ and ⌘] (or the toolbar arrows) open the previous or next visible preview; panels stay as arranged and zoom returns to Fit. The **View** control switches between **Colour**, **Values** (greyscale) and **Notan** (three values), also ⌘1–3. Studies only change the displayed preview; exports stay in colour. **Palette** shows five main colours of the displayed preview; click a swatch to copy its hex value, or **Copy** for all five.
4. **Previews** (Esc) returns to the grid, selects that preview, and commits the edits to that image. Reopening it restores those edits. **Reset preview** restores the generated image and is undoable. The comparison toolbar toggle shows the generated original without discarding adjustments.
5. **Export picks** writes adjusted PNGs at 2400 pixels on the longest side, plus a contact sheet rendered from those exports, with a palette strip under each image. Each export gets a new `Meld picks <date time>` subfolder. The refinement toolbar also exports one PNG (⌘E). **Copy Image** (⇧⌘C) puts a 2400-pixel PNG on the clipboard; dragging a grid thumbnail into Finder or another app provides the same file (temporary drag files are removed after ten minutes).

**More controls** reveals the layer sidebar and detailed Layer/Canvas inspector. Drag layers to reorder them; right-click to duplicate or remove them. Add images (⌘I) or patterns, expand Placement for crop/rotation/position/stretch/mirrored tiling, or adjust individual distortions. An image layer has a **Mask** (bright areas, dark areas, linear or radial fade) with threshold or position, softness and fade angle; the radial centre is shown but not edited. The Canvas panel adds mirror symmetry and its axes, a colour treatment (gradient map, palette swap, posterize as value bands, hue shift) with its strength, levels or angle, motion or zoom blur with length, mix and angle, and grain; gradient and palette-swap colours are shown but not edited. Blend offers overlay, hard light, colour dodge and burn, difference, exclusion, luminosity and colour as well. **Simple controls** hides that workspace again.

The floating glass **Fit · zoom · %** capsule, scroll/pinch panning, and full-resolution magnified preview remain available in refinement. At 100%, one export pixel maps to one physical display pixel. The toolbar can hide or restore the inspector.

Undo/redo stays within the currently opened preview. Navigation commits edits and starts a fresh adjustment history when another preview opens, preventing undo from applying one image's layers to another. Stars and refinements are independent.

**Choose material** returns to source selection and cancels ongoing generation. Existing previews stay until the first result of a new set succeeds. **New 36 previews** replaces the set but keeps earlier picks in their own rows below it. Preview numbers are unique for the session, so picks from different rows export without name collisions. Stopping generation keeps completed previews. Stopping export keeps completed PNGs and their contact sheet.

Meld is a one-session image playground. Export the images you want to keep; the canvas is not saved between launches, and closing the app has no save prompt. Source-folder preferences are still remembered. Older `.meld` files are left untouched but no longer opened by this app.

Everything runs locally. No accounts or network services.

This draft imports images supported by macOS Image I/O, including PNG, JPEG, TIFF, HEIC, GIF, and BMP. Animated images use a single frame. Image layers fill the canvas initially; use Scale and Placement to adjust the crop. Imported sources are limited to 3200 pixels on their longest side. The draft has no brush painting, layer masks, or selection tools.

RAW files (such as NEF, ARW, CR2/CR3, RAF, RW2, ORF, and DNG) are candidates for folder mixing and can also be added directly. Meld uses Apple's [native RAW filter](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coreimage/cirawfilter/init(imageurl:)) with a reduced working size. If development fails, it tries the embedded camera preview. Camera support depends on macOS; extensions alone don't guarantee a file will open. Unreadable files are skipped while finding usable material. Batch generation samples up to 64 candidates to find usable material. If none open, the existing experiment is preserved.

Folder scans only collect file locations; selected images are decoded in the background. Changing folders cancels obsolete scans and material gathering. Each generation rescans so newly added files are included. Hidden files, package contents, and symbolic links are skipped. Source files are never written to. Hover over an image layer to see its source path. Decoded working images stay in memory for the current session.

Each batch samples up to 12 usable sources from at most 64 candidates, decoding each once at a maximum of 2400 pixels. Three decodes run at a time, ahead of rendering, and never more than the pool can use. The pool keeps sampled order, so each group sees the same sources a one-at-a-time decode would give. It renders the first six previews after finding three usable sources (or fewer in a small folder), then broadens the material pool between groups of six. Previews render three at a time on one shared context and appear in number order. Every group of six explores all five recipe families (Flow, Vortex, Blocks, Detail, Tiles) plus one rotating extra. Recipes use deterministic seeded randomness and source-derived palettes; seeds are internal, with no extra settings in the interface. Recipes place every photo full-bleed: a random rotation (any angle, with small tilts and quarter turns slightly favoured), a zoom over the scale that just covers the canvas at that angle (a fifth plain crops, two fifths 1.2–1.9×, two fifths 2–3.6×, so recognisable subjects often dissolve; at most 4× overall unless coverage needs more), and a random point of the image moved to the centre (zoomed crops keep the best of four points by the value contrast of a coarse luminance grid, so detail crops read as shapes rather than flat darkness), then pulled back until the canvas stays covered with a 2% margin. Rotated patterns are enlarged the same way. Each recipe then adds independent stages from four families, each turning photographs into a painting idea rather than a filtered photo: geometry (mirror symmetry across one or both axes near the centre, 25%; a 1.5–3× one-axis stretch into colour bands, 20%), layering (a luminosity-masked double exposure, 25%; a soft linear or radial fade between photos, 22%; an echo of the base photo over itself, larger and turned, 12%; a contrasting blend such as overlay, hard light, colour or luminosity, with occasional difference, exclusion or dodge, 16%), colour (a duotone or tritone gradient map from the source palette, 24%; another source's palette over the original values, 18%; posterized value bands, 14%; a small hue shift of 8–25°, 12%) and surface (motion blur, 20%, or a gentler zoom blur from the warp centre, 8%, softened first and partly mixed so they read as colour fields while the shapes stay; fine grain as surface texture, 12%). The percentages apply to ordinary previews. A budget allows at most two strong stages (mirror, stretch, luminosity mask, gradient map, posterize, a blur or a wild blend) and three in all, counting medium stages as half. Plans are chosen per set: no combination repeats within a set, previews in a group of six share at most one stage choice, and each group has at least one quiet preview (base style plus one stage), about one in five overall. The base photograph always stays whole, opaque and unmasked. Patterns are only a faint texture over the photographs: dots, checks, rings or noise (no stripes), soft light or multiply, at most 30% opacity, also after variations. Detail shows one strongly zoomed crop under faint full-bleed glazes of the other sources and a fine grain. Variations re-apply coverage after their nudges; manual Scale and Placement edits stay unconstrained.

Recipes, edits and undo share immutable decoded working images. Loading no longer compresses sources into intermediate PNGs, and palette extraction uses an already decoded thumbnail. Each source has a reusable 1000-pixel preview and a small sidebar image. RAWs use the same native development for these previews and their retained full working copy, avoiding differences between camera JPEG previews and RAW exports. The camera preview remains a fallback when native development fails. Exports remain independent of the source folder after loading.

Trials keep 480-pixel thumbnails. Refinement starts at 1000 pixels and switches to 2400 pixels when magnified. During adjustments, one 640-pixel render runs at a time and one pending request holds the latest values; the final image returns to the requested resolution after input settles. Opening another preview resets to Fit. Sidebar thumbnails are reused when unrelated controls change. Core Image contexts are reused and intermediate caching is disabled. Render contexts have a 128 MB render-task memory target; this is not a cap on total process memory. The material decoding context has no target: tiling native RAW development under one roughly doubled its time and raised the footprint. Mirrored tiles are shrunk to their on-canvas size, then repeated by a small mirror warp kernel that samples the edge-clamped cell, so rotated tiles have no seams. The Flow displacement map is enlarged by Core Image rather than on the CPU. Noise patterns are written straight into their bitmap instead of filling up to 230,000 cells one Core Graphics call at a time, which took up to a second per 480-pixel preview; partly covered edge pixels match those fills to within a few levels, invisible under the faint texture. Pixellation samples the edge-clamped composite, so its border blocks stay opaque. Mirrored tiles, including manual ones, clamp their cell from one pixel inside its edge, so no resampled edge lets the background through; this slightly changes the outermost pixels of each repeat. Mirror symmetry crops the larger side and reflects it across a whole-pixel axis; blurs and posterize smoothing sample the edge-clamped composite, and blurs run at most 800 pixels across before being enlarged, which keeps 2400-pixel exports fast and within memory; masks and colour treatments are small colour kernels. All stage radii scale with the long edge, so previews and 2400-pixel exports match.

Cancellation is checked during folder traversal, between decoding stages, and between renders; export also checks before PNG compression and before writing. Individual native decode/render calls finish before stopping. Late callbacks cannot restore a cancelled batch or a previous refinement visit. Both single-image and batch export use a separate serial queue, independent of generation and interactive rendering. Batch export encodes up to four PNGs at a time with ImageIO while their 2400-pixel renders take turns; the contact sheet keeps the chosen order. Batch export uses a unique destination subfolder; existing images are never overwritten. Trials and favourites are held only for the session.

## Build

Apple Silicon Mac and macOS 14+ to run. Build with Apple command-line developer tools containing a macOS 26+ SDK (older OS versions use native fallbacks):

```sh
zsh build.sh
open Meld.app
```

The app uses SwiftUI, AppKit, and Core Image. It is signed locally with an ad hoc signature.

From the repository root, run the rendering and session checks from a normal Mac terminal (Core Image needs access to the graphics system):

```sh
./Meld.app/Contents/MacOS/Meld --self-test /private/tmp/meld-checks
```

The checks cover native slider resets, zoom geometry and preview resolution, layer commands, patterns, blending, every distortion, comparison, visibility, opacity, placement, canvas shapes, undo/redo, new-canvas undo, drag reordering and its undo/redo, legacy-project rejection, PNG export, recursive folder scanning, source preservation, corrupt files, fresh random selection, background-edit protection, image orientation, and bounded decoding. Workflow checks cover 36 distinct previews, balanced styles, source-first startup, refinement scaling and undo, per-image edit retention, reset and original comparison, picks, adjusted full-size PNGs and contact sheets, cancellation, source disconnection, and preserving previous previews when a folder fails. Performance regressions additionally cover shared image ownership and release, progressive material loading, continuous adjustment feedback and final pixel accuracy, stale preview rejection, Fit reset after magnification, concurrent export snapshots, cancellation, wide-gamut colour and alpha, and source changes between folder selection and generation. Full-bleed checks compare the cover-scale formula with a brute-force corner test and confirm, across every style, shape and variation strength, that generated layers cover the canvas geometrically and that solid test photos never reveal a garish background, with or without distortions. They also confirm that generated patterns and repeated variations keep within the texture limits, and that mirror, stretch, masks, fades, echoes, blurs and grain never reveal the background or leave transparency. Stage checks cover stage frequencies against their probabilities and the quiet share over many seeds, the intensity budget for generated and repeatedly varied recipes, every stage occurring across a few sets, unique combinations per set and limited overlap within each group of six, recipes containing exactly their planned stages, nudges and stage swaps in variations, deterministic plans and staged renders, each refinement control at 0 turning its stages off while 100% restores the recipe, undo and redo of stage settings, and 2400-pixel renders of the heaviest combinations. Pipeline checks cover ordered concurrent delivery and stopping, the ordered material pool and its bound, bounded memory for mirrored tiles, progressive and numbered concurrent previews, parallel export matching serial pixels, mid-batch export cancellation without late writes, and 24-hour export folder names. The optional RAW check also generates 36 previews from one real NEF and checks rendering after removing a temporary RAW copy. Background waits fail after 60 seconds without a finished render, so a throttled machine (for example on battery) can take longer while a real hang still fails.

To also check a real RAW image without modifying it:

```sh
MELD_RAW_FIXTURE=/path/to/photo.NEF ./Meld.app/Contents/MacOS/Meld --self-test /private/tmp/meld-checks
```

RAW validation during development used the [rawpy Nikon NEF fixture](https://github.com/letmaik/rawpy/blob/main/test/iss030e122639.NEF), downloaded into a temporary folder only. No sample photography is bundled with the app.

## Interface direction

The main workspace follows source → previews → refinement. Source selection uses a quiet central folder chooser and one prominent generation action. The preview grid owns the full window, with a native toolbar matching Louppe: source folder at the left, compact status in the center, and grouped Picks, Variations, generation, and export icons at the right. Generation and export expose Stop in their respective toolbar positions while running. Full status messages remain available in the center tooltip. Refinement preserves the native toolbar, teal accent, right inspector, and floating glass zoom; the layer sidebar is hidden until More controls is requested. Glass buttons have native bordered fallbacks before macOS 26. The minimum window remains 1050 × 700 points.

To time real model operations on a folder (read only): `./Meld.app/Contents/MacOS/Meld --benchmark /path/to/folder 3` prints the first preview, all 36, opening a preview, exporting it, exporting six picks and making 12 variations, per run and as medians, with the peak memory footprint after the set and after the exports. Run *n* always uses the same seeds, so separate builds repeat the same work; compare them on the same power source, as battery power and thermal throttling slow every step.

To inspect the interface without activating Meld or moving focus: `./Meld.app/Contents/MacOS/Meld --ui-snapshot /private/tmp/meld-ui /path/to/folder` renders the real window offscreen and writes PNGs of source selection, generation, the grid with and without picks, variations (light and dark, including minimum width), export progress and status, refinement, Notan, return navigation, and More controls (Canvas and a masked Layer) in a taller window. It exports disposable previews into the output directory and cancels that export after capturing progress. Liquid Glass backgrounds don't appear in these captures, and an empty placeholder circle sits where the hidden sidebar toggle would be; both are capture artefacts.

For UI checks without restoring or changing the saved source preference, launch the executable with `--ui-test`. Add `--ui-test-source /path/to/test/folder` to select a fixture folder without scanning it at startup. All `--self-test` studios likewise use `Studio(restoreSource: false)`.
